# Examplimab versus placebo in biologic-naive active psoriatic arthritis: the FICTA-1 randomized trial

Doe J, Roe R, Example A. *Journal of Synthetic Examples.* 2025;1:1–10.

## Abstract
**Background.** Many patients with psoriatic arthritis (PsA) do not reach low disease activity on current therapy. Examplimab is a fictional monoclonal antibody against the fictional cytokine IL-X.
**Methods.** Single-country, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. 240 biologic-naive adults with active PsA were randomized 1:1 to examplimab or placebo for 24 weeks. Primary endpoint: ACR20 response at week 24.
**Results.** ACR20 at week 24 was reached by 58% (70/120) with examplimab and 31% (37/120) with placebo; difference 27 percentage points (95% CI 15 to 39; p<0.001).
**Conclusion.** Examplimab improved joint outcomes over 24 weeks. Longer studies with radiographic outcomes are needed.

## Introduction
Fewer than half of patients with PsA reach minimal disease activity on their first biologic (reviewed in refs 3–5 of this fictional paper).

## Methods
- Population: biologic-naive adults, ≥3 tender and ≥3 swollen joints, inadequate response to at least one conventional DMARD.
- Randomization 1:1, stratified by methotrexate use. Treatment 24 weeks.
- Primary endpoint: ACR20 at week 24 (non-responder imputation).
- Secondary: change in HAQ-DI; PASI75 in patients with ≥3% body surface area psoriasis at baseline (n = 150).
- Pre-specified safety: serious infections. A CRP subgroup analysis was **post hoc**.

## Discussion
Examplimab improved signs, symptoms and physical function over 24 weeks. The trial was not designed to assess radiographic progression, enrolled only biologic-naive patients in one country, and the CRP subgroup finding is post hoc and hypothesis-generating. Serious infections were numerically more frequent with examplimab; the trial was not powered to compare safety.
